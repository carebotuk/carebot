## Every supply run takes someone away from another job

Fresh linen needed at a delivery point. Everyday supplies to replenish. Trays to move across a dining area. Across a large building, repeated journeys can occupy a substantial part of a support team's day.

A service robot can take on the carrying along an agreed route. We help you identify a useful starting point, understand the handover at each end and assess whether it fits the way your hospital operates.

## Two robots for different support tasks

**FlashBot Max: enclosed linen and supply deliveries.** Staff load its compartments and send it to a mapped destination, where a colleague receives the items. Consider it for repeat trips from stores to agreed handover points. Travel between floors depends on compatible lift integration and the route assessment.

**KettyBot Pro: tray carrying in dining areas.** In a staff restaurant or visitor café, open trays can help move dishes between the kitchen and service points. Staff load, unload and serve; the robot helps with the repeated carrying.

The starting point is routine non-clinical work, with each proposed use assessed for your setting.

## Planned around a working hospital

Your delivery routes are shared with people, equipment and other essential services. We assess corridors, doors, lifts, floor surfaces, connectivity and charging locations with your team, including how those spaces change during busy periods.

We involve the relevant operational, facilities, IT and infection prevention and control teams in reviewing the proposed use. Cleaning requirements, access permissions, handover responsibilities and arrangements when the robot is unavailable form part of that discussion.

## One UK partner from assessment to support

Carebot UK handles equipment selection, installation, route mapping and staff training. We agree the scope and support arrangements before installation, so your team knows what is included and who to contact afterwards.

Start with one repeat journey: what is carried, how often it happens and who makes the trip. Bring a floor plan or route outline to a demonstration, and we can explore the relevant model and the next steps together.
